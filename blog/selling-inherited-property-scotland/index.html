Selling an Inherited Property in Scotland (2026 Guide)

By SB Properties UK • Content updated: 11 October 2026

Losing a loved one is incredibly difficult, and dealing with their property can feel overwhelming. In Scotland, the legal process for handling an estate is unique and different from England or Wales.

Whether the estate includes a Glasgow flat or an inherited house in Paisley, start by establishing who can act for the estate, what the property needs and which sale route fits the estate's responsibilities.

What is Confirmation in Scotland?

Confirmation is the Scottish court process that can give an executor authority to deal with estate assets. It is often compared with probate in England and Wales, but the rules differ. Ask the estate solicitor whether Confirmation is required and who has authority to sell this particular property.

1. The Initial Valuation

The executor must prepare the estate information and valuations required for Confirmation and tax purposes. Ask the estate solicitor what evidence is needed; a buyer's informal assessment is not an independent estate valuation.

2. Submitting Forms to the Sheriff Court

The information and forms depend on the estate and its tax position. Prepare the inventory with appropriate advice and use the current Scottish Courts and HMRC requirements. Avoid promising a sale date before the estate solicitor confirms the necessary steps and likely timing.

Can you sell the house before Confirmation is granted?

You can discuss potential sale options while the estate is being administered. Whether the property can be marketed, a binding contract agreed or ownership transferred at a particular stage depends on title and authority. Ask the estate solicitor before making commitments; a buyer's offer does not replace Confirmation where it is required.

Common Roadblocks for Inherited Sales

These practical issues can affect an inherited sale through any route:

  • The "Clean-Out" Burden: Decades of belongings can take months to clear.
  • Condition Issues: Many inherited properties need significant modernization (new boilers, wiring, or damp treatment).
  • Reports and repairs: A Home Report is normally needed for public marketing. Condition issues can affect value and mortgage availability. A cash offer is not itself an exemption.
  • Sibling Disputes: If multiple beneficiaries are involved, a long, drawn-out sale can create tension.

For practical next steps on how to manage these challenges, read our comprehensive guide on clearing and selling an inherited house in Scotland.

When a direct sale may suit an inherited property

A direct offer may be useful for a property needing work or with contents remaining. It may also be lower than an open-market offer. Executors should take advice about their responsibilities and compare the price, costs and conditions before deciding.

  1. Home Report: ask your solicitor which requirements apply to the marketing and proposed transaction.
  2. Net proceeds: Compare the offer after legal costs, any existing agency obligations and other agreed charges. Ask for all terms in writing.
  3. Contents: Tell the buyer what would remain and agree any clearance arrangement in writing. Confirm authority before disposing of estate belongings.
  4. Buyer and timing: SB Properties may buy directly or introduce an investor with your permission. Any purchase depends on assessment, agreement and legal checks.
