How to Sell a House Privately in Scotland

By SB Properties UK • Content updated: 11 October 2026

Selling a house privately in Scotland is entirely legal. You are not required by law to use an estate agent to market your property.

A private sale can reduce the marketing work you pay an agent to do, but it still needs careful preparation, legal checks and buyer assessment. Review any existing agency agreement: commission or other charges may still be due even if you find the buyer yourself.

This guide explains the main steps and questions for a private Scottish sale. The documents, costs and legal arrangements depend on the property and transaction, so confirm them with your solicitor before committing.

Can you legally sell without an estate agent in Scotland?

You can choose to find a buyer yourself. Before advertising, confirm the Home Report and other marketing requirements with your solicitor. You will also need to arrange enquiries, viewings and the handling of offers.

Instruct a Scottish solicitor early to advise on the legal transfer, missives and settlement arrangements. Selling without an estate agent does not remove the need to deal correctly with title, mortgage discharge and the contract.

What does a private house sale actually mean?

Before proceeding, it is vital to understand that "selling privately" is a broad term that describes several very different routes. The legal requirements vary significantly depending on which route you take:

  1. Self-Marketing (For Sale By Owner): You advertise the property yourself online or locally to the public, finding a stranger to buy it.
  2. Selling to a Known Individual: You agree a price with a family member, a friend, or an existing tenant without ever advertising the property.
  3. Direct purchase or investor introduction: You discuss a potential sale with a buyer or a business that may introduce an investor. Establish who will buy and how the property will be presented; the label off-market does not settle the legal requirements.

These routes have different marketing and buyer-finding arrangements. All still require the legal position, buyer funding and contract terms to be checked.

Do you still need a solicitor?

Speak to a Scottish solicitor before agreeing a private sale, including one to a relative or tenant. Ask them to explain the work, costs, independence and representation needed for your transaction.

A solicitor can handle the missives, title investigation and settlement arrangements, including any mortgage discharge and relevant identity or source-of-funds checks. Ask which requirements apply and avoid treating an informal price agreement as a completed legal contract.

Do you need a Home Report for a private sale?

The most common point of confusion in a Scottish private sale is the Home Report.

When a Home Report is normally required

A Home Report is normally needed when a Scottish home is marketed. The documents generally need to be no more than 12 weeks old when marketing starts, subject to the applicable rules and exceptions. Check your circumstances before advertising.

"Marketing" includes:

  • Uploading the property to a portal or website.
  • Putting a "For Sale" sign in the window.
  • Posting about the sale on public social media groups.
  • Distributing flyers.

These activities can amount to marketing even when no estate agent is involved. Establish the requirements first and obtain a surveyor's quote if a Home Report is needed.

Potential private and off-market exemptions

A genuinely unmarketed private transaction may be outside the Home Report requirement. Check the marketing history and proposed route with your solicitor before relying on this.

  • Sell to a family member or friend in a private agreement.
  • Sell to a sitting tenant.
  • Sell off-market to a direct cash buyer or property developer.

Why sellers should verify their circumstances

Cash payment, a family relationship or a tenancy is not, by itself, a Home Report exemption. Check the marketing history and proposed transaction with your solicitor. Read our guide to Home Report requirements for the distinction.

How to sell privately in Scotland step by step

If you decide to proceed without an estate agent, you must manage the sale process yourself. Here is how a Scottish transaction typically flows.

Decide how the buyer will be found

Decide whether you will advertise for buyers or discuss a transaction with someone already identified. Tell your solicitor about previous marketing and any proposed investor introductions, then confirm documents and viewing arrangements.

Establish a realistic property value

Use relevant completed sales and an independent professional opinion to assess value, rather than relying only on asking prices. A Single Survey includes a surveyor's valuation, but the final sale price depends on the offers and conditions agreed.

Prepare the required information and documents

Gather your title deeds (if held locally), any building warrants or completion certificates for past alterations, and guarantees for timber treatments or damp proofing.

Confirm the buyer’s identity and funding

Establish who the buyer is, their funding source, any chain and conditions. A mortgage lender may require an acceptable valuation or additional reports; this is separate from the statutory Home Report rules. Ask your solicitor how funds and identity will be checked.

Instruct a Scottish solicitor

Instruct a Scottish solicitor to advise on the transaction. Ask about conflicts of interest and appropriate independent representation, including where the buyer is a relative.

Receive and negotiate a formal offer

In a typical transaction, the buyer's solicitor sends a written offer covering price, date of entry and conditions. Your solicitor discusses it with you and negotiates the terms. Ask whether any acceptance concludes a binding contract before instructing it.

Conclude missives

Concluded missives create a binding contract subject to its terms. Ask your solicitor to explain any conditions, withdrawal rights and possible consequences before committing.

Complete conveyancing and settlement

The solicitors arrange the disposition, funds and other completion requirements. Follow your solicitor's confirmation about settlement and when keys can be released; the details depend on the agreed contract.

Repay the mortgage and transfer ownership

Ask your solicitor for a completion statement showing the price, mortgage redemption, fees and other deductions. Any shortfall needs a workable arrangement with the lender; the price offered is not necessarily the amount you retain.

Documents a private seller may need

Ask your solicitor which documents they need. The following are common examples; requirements depend on the property and transaction. Gathering available records early can help identify missing information:

  • Proof of Identity: Photographic ID and recent utility bills for AML checks.
  • Title Deeds: If your property is not yet on the digital Land Register.
  • Home Report and EPC: Check which documents are required for the marketing and transaction.
  • Alteration Documents: Building warrants, planning permissions, and completion certificates.
  • Specialist Guarantees: Paperwork for damp proofing, timber treatments, or roof repairs.
  • Factoring Information: Details of your property factor and any upcoming communal repair bills.
  • Mortgage Details: Your lender’s name and account number for a redemption statement.

Costs of selling privately

Get written quotes and compare the net proceeds. Avoid assuming a private transaction has no agency liability if you have already instructed an agent. Costs to check include:

  • Legal costs: Obtain a quote covering the work, VAT and any additional expenses.
  • Home Report or other reports: Confirm requirements and obtain quotes.
  • Marketing and viewings: Check any portal, photography or accompanied-viewing charges.
  • Holding costs: Budget for mortgage interest, insurance, Council Tax and maintenance as applicable until the relevant liabilities end.

Risks and common mistakes

  • Pricing: Compare relevant sold prices and obtain professional advice on the property's current condition and value.
  • Buyer funding: Verify the proposed funding and outstanding conditions rather than relying only on the headline offer.
  • Title and alterations: Discuss missing consents, certificates or title information early; they may require further advice or work.
  • Payment security: Ask your solicitor how deposits and settlement funds should be handled. Independently verify payment instructions using a trusted contact method before transferring money; do not rely on unexpected emails.

How long can a private sale take?

A buyer with verified funds may avoid a mortgage application, but title, authority, contract conditions and other legal work still determine what is achievable. Agree a proposed date through the solicitor rather than assuming a fixed number of weeks.

If you need to find a buyer, allow for marketing as well as legal completion. Review interest and the outstanding steps regularly, and budget for a delay. Read our guide to planning a Glasgow sale timetable.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use the same solicitor for a private house sale in Scotland?

Ask your solicitor about conflicts and appropriate independent representation. Do not assume that sharing a firm or selling to a relative removes the need for the proper legal arrangements.

Do I need a Home Report to sell to my tenant?

A genuinely unmarketed sale may not require a Home Report. Tell your solicitor about any advertising and confirm other requirements, including the EPC, for the proposed tenant purchase.

How do I value my house for a private sale?

You can look at recent sold prices on the Registers of Scotland, instruct a chartered surveyor for an independent valuation, or commission a formal Home Report.

Compare your plan with a direct or introduced offer

If you are considering a private sale to reduce preparation or explore another route for a difficult property, compare a direct or introduced investor offer with broader marketing. Assess the net proceeds, funding, conditions and timing of each option.
